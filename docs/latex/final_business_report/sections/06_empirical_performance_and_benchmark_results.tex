\section{Empirical Performance and Benchmark Results}
\label{sec:empirical_results}

Under the formal \texttt{fair-eval-v1} protocol, exhaustive empirical evaluations were executed across all 15 MVTec AD categories, with operational decision thresholds calibrated exclusively on held-out normal validation partitions. The results documented below characterise the production-grade reference architecture: PatchCore utilising a frozen ResNet-18 backbone, representing the optimal convergence of defect discriminability, deterministic edge latency, and hardware efficiency.

\subsection{Category-Level Performance Breakdown}

Table~\ref{tab:category_results} provides the disaggregated performance across all 15 industrial categories. Evaluated under the default balanced $F_1$ threshold, the platform achieves a macro-average Image $F_1$-score of $0.929$, an Image PR-AUC of $0.988$, and a pixel-level AUPIMO of $0.603$. Defect recall across the benchmark averages $0.928$ with an average precision of $0.940$. When operational risk-tier adjustments are applied at line commissioning, these operating points shift deterministically in accordance with the client's cost-of-quality model.

\subsection{Engineering Analysis of Boundary Categories}

The two categories presenting the most pronounced economic asymmetry -- pharmaceutical tablets (\emph{Pill}) and threaded fasteners (\emph{Screw}) -- represent opposing poles of the empirical performance spectrum and warrant detailed engineering scrutiny.

Under balanced default calibration, the \emph{Pill} category attains an Image $F_1$-score of $0.891$ and PR-AUC of $0.982$, with default recall at $0.816$. In a life-critical pharmaceutical facility, an $18.4\%$ defect escape rate is legally and commercially intolerable. However, when configured under Tier 1 calibration ($F_2$-optimised objective), the operational threshold is adjusted to capture the nominal tail: defect recall reaches $1.000$ whilst maintaining precision above $89\%$. In operational terms, every defective tablet within the evaluation split is intercepted and diverted to secondary inspection, whilst fewer than one in nine diverted units constitutes a false alarm. This operational profile fulfills strict regulatory compliance (such as GMP and FDA inspection mandates) whilst bounding the manual secondary audit volume to a commercially viable overhead.

Conversely, the \emph{Screw} category presents the most severe technical challenge across the entire benchmark, recording an Image $F_1$-score of $0.796$ and AUPIMO of $0.380$. This performance ceiling is rooted in component physics: screws are acquired under arbitrary rotational orientations in single-channel greyscale, and anomalous manifestations -- such as microscopic thread swarf, minor pitch irregularities, or minute tip shears -- produce feature embeddings that overlap substantially with the nominal distribution. Because ResNet-18 residual features operate across receptive fields designed for natural imagery, they lack native inductive bias for sub-millimetre helical thread geometries. Crucially, when evaluating pixel-level localisation on \emph{Screw}, absolute precision values must be contextualised against the extreme class imbalance: a trivial majority-class (always-nominal) classifier yields a Pixel Average Precision of merely $0.0025$, reflecting the exact random chance baseline for sub-millimetre thread defects. Against this no-skill reference, the non-learned baseline achieves a $5\times$ lift over chance ($0.0126$), whilst the PatchCore feature-matching engine achieves over a $20\times$ lift over chance. Under Tier 3 calibration ($F_{0.5}$ objective), the system prioritises precision ($0.976$), ensuring that automated driving stations are not plagued by pseudo-scrap whilst reliably catching catastrophic structural failures (such as sheared shanks or absent threading). Given the bounded commercial cost of single fastener escapes, this trade-off is economically optimal.

\subsection{Texture Category Performance and Spatial Localisation Quality}

Continuous texture categories systematically record the highest spatial localisation fidelity under the AUPIMO metric. \emph{Leather} ($0.972$), \emph{Carpet} ($0.796$), and \emph{Tile} ($0.685$) benefit from stationary surface statistics, generating intense patch-distance contrast between nominal weave patterns and localised structural disruptions (such as surface cuts or voids). Even categories exhibiting higher natural stochasticity -- such as \emph{Wood} ($0.642$) and \emph{Grid} ($0.506$) -- maintain Image PR-AUC values exceeding $0.985$. This demonstrates that even when intricate material grain marginally broadens localised heatmaps, image-level classification and divert reliability remain exceptionally robust.
